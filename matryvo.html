Context

The problem wasn't sourcing. It was uncertainty.

Matryvo connects building projects with factories in China and handles specifications, payments, production, quality checks, shipping and delivery. Buying across borders stacks up risk: unclear supplier accountability, uneven quality, fragmented logistics, payment anxiety and little visibility between order and delivery.

Matryvo had to present itself as more than a sourcing company. The brand needed to communicate control, trust and a clear process.

  1. Rising cost

    Every intermediary adds margin and eats into the budget.

  2. Supplier risk

    Clients need confidence that what they specified is what gets made.

  3. Limited visibility

    Long procurement cycles feel risky when clients can't see progress.

  4. No clear owner

    When something changes, clients need one person accountable.

Design objective

Make a complicated process feel simple, not simplistic.

Exact

Specs, quantities, payments and dates should feel document-grade, not promotional.

Calm

Hierarchy, whitespace and predictable structure reduce anxiety.

Grounded

The look should feel architectural and material, not like a glossy tech startup.

Accountable

Every project state makes the next step and the responsible person obvious.

Strategy

Four decisions shaped the system.

  1. Journey before catalogue

    The website explains the procurement path first, instead of opening with a giant product list.

  2. Evidence before marketing

    Bills of quantities, QA records, stages, dates and named owners earn more trust than generic claims.

  3. One accent, one priority

    Laterite orange is reserved for the single most important action on each screen.

  4. Editorial clarity

    Structured type and architecture-inspired spacing make dense information easy to scan.

Core promise

You always know what you ordered, what you paid, what's next, and who to call.

That sentence became the design filter for both the brand and the product.

Identity

A mark about holding the project together.

Two architectural arches form a lowercase m. Smaller laterite arches sit inside them: the quote, deposit and order that Matryvo manages within the client's larger project.

matryvo

  1. Outer arches

    The client's project: the larger structure being built.

  2. Inner arches

    The pieces Matryvo runs for them: quote, deposit and order.

  3. Lowercase wordmark

    Approachable and modern, with the authority of Bricolage Grotesque SemiBold.

  4. Restrained accent

    Laterite is recognisable without turning every surface into a brand-coloured block.
