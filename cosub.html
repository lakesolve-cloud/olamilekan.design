Case study · Subscriptions · Mobile app

Cosub.

Splitting Netflix and Spotify with friends is common in Nigeria, but it runs on WhatsApp groups and trust. I designed Cosub to make sharing, joining and paying for subscriptions one simple flow.

RoleLead Product Designer
Duration4 months
PlatformMobile app
Research55 surveyed · 15 interviewed
The problem

Subscriptions keep getting pricier. Sharing is messy.

With rising subscription costs in Nigeria and no good way to manage a shared plan, people struggle to:

  • Keep track of several subscriptions across different platforms
  • Remember renewal dates and payment deadlines
  • Split costs with friends or family without chasing them for money

The result is overpaying, plans that lapse mid-show, and money wasted on renewals nobody wanted.

What research showed

Sharing already happens. It just isn't organised.

73%have more than one digital subscription
68%already share at least one subscription informally

Information architecture

Two jobs from one home screen.

Everyone either shares a plan they pay for or joins someone else's. Home branches straight into both, with chat and profile alongside.

Sign up / Log in→Home→
Share a subscription→Set plan details→Confirm & publish
Join a subscription→Browse open groups→Join & pay
Chat→Group chats
Profile→Edit profile & settings
Key design decisions

What the research turned into.

Share or join, chosen up front

Right after sign-up, people pick whether they want to share a plan they own or join one. The rest of the app adapts to that choice, so nobody wades through features meant for the other side.

A home built around money due

Home splits into Shared subs, Joined subs and Upcoming payments, with clear "Due" and date tags. It answers the 80% who forget renewals before they have to go looking.

Short, guided sharing flow

Sharing a plan takes four small steps: category, brand, plan details, then review. Owners can mark a group private or public, which keeps the trust decision in their hands.

Group chat replaces WhatsApp

Every shared plan gets its own chat for logins and one-time passcodes. It moves the 68% who already share informally into a space linked to the plan and its payments.

Visible payment status

The group profile shows each member and whether they've paid, so owners stop chasing friends for money and members can see they're in good standing.

Visual language

Quiet UI, loud brands.

A neutral black-and-white interface set in Rocket Sans lets the Netflix, Spotify and Prime logos carry the colour, so people spot their subscriptions at a glance.
